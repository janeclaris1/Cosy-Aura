## Core takes

- Watches are **water-resistant at best** — not waterproof. Nothing withstands absolute real-world pressure forever.
- No brand truly claims a totally waterproof watch; that claim is essentially impossible (and legally risky).
- The metres/feet rating on a dial or caseback is **not literal swimming depth** — a common beginner mistake.
- Lab tests (often dry/static hyperbaric air tests) do not match real wear: swimming, temperature swings, moving water, G-forces.
- Ratings still matter as a **rough consumer guide**, even when imperfect.
- Many brands **understate** resistance for conservatism (e.g. Panerai testing to ~125% of stated rating).
- Patek Philippe’s Aquanaut WR drop (120 m → 30 m) is best read as **conservatism**, not a sudden weaker watch — though you still shouldn’t assume that when shopping “average” pieces.
- **Leather straps + water** is a bad mix even on dive watches; salt water especially destroys leather fast. Mixed messaging when a dive watch ships on leather.
- Taking most watches into the **shower** below ~200 m WR is a bad idea: steam, heat, and gasket stress. Wearing a watch in the shower is generally silly.
- 30 m and 50 m are closer cousins than marketing suggests — both are splash territory, not swimming territory.
- 100 m is where sports watches get serious for swimming/snorkeling — still not “dive to printed depth.”
- 200 m is typically where **real dive-watch intent** starts (swim, surf, snorkel, skin dive); not saturation diving.

## Tone / voice cues

- Direct TL;DR lines after each rating.
- Mild sarcasm toward marketing vs reality.
- Personal authority: “from someone who’s owned (and swam) with enough watches to know.”
- Call out brand quirks without being purely hostile (Panerai leather, Patek WR downgrade).

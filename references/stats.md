## Rating cheat sheet

| Stated rating | ATM / Bar | Real-world meaning (practical) |
|---------------|-----------|--------------------------------|
| **30 m** | 3 ATM / 3 Bar | Splashes, hand washing, light rain. Not swimming. Not shower. |
| **50 m** | 5 ATM / 5 Bar | Similar to 30 m: splashes OK; swimming not. Shower still risky. |
| **100 m** | 10 ATM / 10 Bar | Swimming and snorkeling OK for most sports watches. Not scuba to printed depth. |
| **200 m** | 20 ATM / 20 Bar | Typical dive-watch territory: swim, surf, snorkel, skin dive. Not saturation diving. |

## Testing & standards

- Most factory WR testing is **dry/static**: hyperbaric chamber, air pressure, check for leaks/deformation — often **no water** touches the watch.
- Water immersion tests (when used) are usually slow and controlled — unlike repeated submerge/surface while swimming.
- Real-world pressure changes (motion, temperature, moving water, G-forces) reduce effective resistance vs lab numbers.
- **Panerai**: often cited as testing to **125%** of stated water resistance.
- **ISO 6425**: dive-watch standard; includes requirements such as static submersion at **125%** of rated resistance; each piece independently tested (costly → uncommon).
- **Blancpain “Fifty Fathoms”**: historically ~**91 m** (50 fathoms); modern Fifty Fathoms often rated **300 m**.

## Brand examples cited in source

- **Patek Philippe Aquanaut** (incl. Travel Time 5164G context): WR standardized to **30 m**, down from **120 m** (noted as ~2024 change) — confused collectors.
- **Richard Mille**: mentioned among brands that have **downgraded** stated WR on some models.
- Common sports-watch floor: many modern sports watches rate at least **100 m**, often with screw-down crown and/or caseback.
- Example product contexts in source: Glashütte Original SeaQ (pressure testing), Tudor Black Bay (immersion test), Doxa SUB 200 (200 m / ETA 2824-2 context), TAG Heuer Carrera Glassbox, AP Royal Oak (50 m class dress/sports-luxury), Vacheron Historiques (30 m class).

## Quick TL;DRs (from source)

- **30 m**: keep away from water  
- **50 m**: nothing more than washing hands or a walk in the rain  
- **100 m**: okay to swim with  
- **200 m**: dive-watch activities OK; not saturation diving
